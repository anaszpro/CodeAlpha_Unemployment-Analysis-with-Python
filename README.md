# Unemployment Analysis & COVID-19 Economic Impact

**Project Context:** Completed as part of the Data Science Internship at CodeAlpha.

## Overview
This project analyzes the macroeconomic impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on the labor market. By exploring time-series data, the analysis identifies key demographic patterns, geographic disparities, and underlying trends to present data-driven insights that can inform future economic and social relief policies.

## Repository Structure
Based on the repository files, here is how to navigate the project:
*   **`Dataset/`**: This directory contains the raw CSV dataset used for the analysis.
*   **`Unemployment_Analysis.ipynb`**: The main Jupyter Notebook containing all the Python code. This includes the structural data cleaning, exploratory data analysis (EDA), and the generation of all data visualizations.
*   **`Unemployment Analysis & COVID-19 Economic Impact.pdf`**: The executive summary report. This document translates the code's outputs into actionable overarching trends, key findings, and policy recommendations designed for business stakeholders.

## Project Objectives
*   Analyze unemployment rate data representing the percentage of unemployed individuals.
*   Utilize Python for data cleaning, mathematical exploration, and visual mapping of time-series trends.
*   Investigate the specific economic shock caused by COVID-19 lockdown measures.
*   Identify distinct demographic patterns (Urban vs. Rural impacts) and state-level vulnerabilities.
*   Translate data into actionable insights for economic policy planning.

## Key Findings
1.  **The Macro Shock:** National unemployment spiked vertically from a pre-pandemic baseline of ~9% to nearly 25% during the peak lockdown period in mid-2020.
2.  **Urban vs. Rural Disparity:** Urban areas suffered disproportionately higher job losses (peaking at ~29%) compared to rural areas (~24%), largely due to the immediate shutdown of retail, hospitality, and corporate sectors.
3.  **State-Level Vulnerability:** Economic impact varied drastically by geographic region, proving that blanket national relief policies are inefficient. Highly impacted regions experienced unemployment nearing 60%, while insulated regions remained under 10%.
